01 / A molecular journey

One pipeline. Different jobs.

A therapeutic hypothesis changes scientific scale as it moves through discovery. These tools are complementary: a genome model is not a structure predictor, and a structure predictor is not a potency assay.

  1. 01 · Genomic contextEvo 2 reads long DNA sequence context to help prioritize variants, regulatory patterns, and hypotheses.
  2. 02 · Protein sequenceESM-family models turn amino-acid sequences into learned representations for function and property tasks.
  3. 03 · Structure and interactionAlphaFold-family models propose a folded structure or biomolecular complex to inspect, not an experimental result.
  4. 04 · Physics and rankingSchrödinger software uses docking, molecular dynamics, and free-energy methods to rank molecular ideas.
  5. 05 · Closed-loop designGenesis GEMS combines proprietary generative and predictive models to propose and optimize compounds for selected targets.
  6. 06 · Wet-lab validationAssays, structural experiments, and medicinal chemistry decide which hypotheses survive.

02 / Five instruments

Five names, five different instruments.

Choose a name to orient the comparison. Every detail remains below as a static fallback, because one selected card should never hide the rest of the scientific context.

AlphaFold family

Technology type
Protein and biomolecular structure prediction.
Input → output
Sequence and related biological context → a predicted three-dimensional structure with confidence information.
Best commercial role
Making a structural hypothesis available early enough to guide target assessment, construct design, and follow-up modeling.
Maturity
Widely used structural starting point; complex and ligand-bound predictions require case-by-case validation.
Important limitation
A predicted static structure does not directly establish affinity, potency, dynamics, ADME, toxicity, synthetic accessibility, or clinical efficacy.

ESM family

Technology type
Protein language models.
Input → output
Amino-acid sequence → embeddings, likelihoods, or task-specific protein predictions.
Best commercial role
Feature generation for protein engineering, functional annotation, mutation prioritization, and downstream predictors.
Maturity
A flexible model family whose utility depends on the task, labels, and evaluation set.
Important limitation
Sequence representations do not substitute for experimental functional measurement and may not transfer to an unseen protein family or assay.

Evo 2

Technology type
Long-context genomic foundation model.
Input → output
DNA or RNA sequence context → learned representations, sequence likelihoods, and generative proposals.
Best commercial role
Connecting genomic variation and regulatory context to target hypotheses before a protein-centric campaign begins.
Maturity
Research and platform evaluation stage for many drug-discovery workflows.
Important limitation
Genomic plausibility is not causal proof of disease mechanism or therapeutic tractability.

Schrödinger

Technology type
Physics-based molecular modeling and drug-discovery software.
Input → output
Prepared structures and molecules → docking poses, molecular-dynamics trajectories, free-energy estimates, and ranked design hypotheses.
Best commercial role
Structure-based design decisions where physical simulation and carefully prepared inputs matter.
Maturity
Established commercial scientific software, licensed across major pharmaceutical companies.
Important limitation
Results depend on model preparation, sampling, force fields, protocol choices, and experimental feedback; they are not direct biological proof.

Genesis GEMS

Technology type
Proprietary generative and predictive AI platform for small-molecule discovery.
Input → output
Target and campaign data → prioritized chemical designs and optimization hypotheses.
Best commercial role
Closed-loop design campaigns that combine proprietary models with medicinal chemistry and experimental data.
Maturity
Partnered platform; public detail is limited relative to open research models.
Important limitation
External users cannot independently inspect the full platform, and generated molecules still require synthesis and wet-lab confirmation.

04 / The structural prior

Where AlphaFold earns its keep.

AlphaFold is most useful when a structural hypothesis removes a bottleneck. Confidence, constructability, ligand state, and orthogonal experimental evidence determine whether a particular prediction is fit for a decision.

Established or mature starting points

  • Target identification and family comparison
  • Target validation and druggability conversations
  • Experimental structure work, including construct design and cryo-EM or crystallography interpretation
  • Early hit discovery when no experimental structure is available

Emerging or experimental uses

  • Complex prediction and interface hypotheses
  • Lead optimization against ligand-bound conformations
  • Biologics design and antibody–antigen complexes
  • Off-target risk hypotheses

Reality check: prediction is not the whole experiment

AlphaFold does not directly provide reliable potency, conformational dynamics, ADME, toxicity, synthetic accessibility, clinical efficacy, or experimental proof. Its output is a model to inspect and test.

Published In a 28-target virtual-screening benchmark, unrefined AlphaFold2 structures had average early enrichment (EF 1%) of 13.16, refined AlphaFold2 structures 19.25, and ligand-bound experimental structures 24.81. The contrast illustrates why binding-site state, refinement, and experimental structures still matter.

Published A prospective TAAR1 screen is a complementary success case: more than 16 million compounds were docked, the AlphaFold-enabled screen reported a 60% hit rate, and it identified potent agonists. A result like this is a reason to test a workflow, not a guarantee that every target will behave similarly.

05 / The closed loop

The working stack closes in the wet lab.

  1. Predicted structure frames a target hypothesis.
  2. Protein-language-model features add sequence-level context.
  3. Docking proposes poses and chemical starting points.
  4. Molecular dynamics and free-energy calculations probe and rank designs.
  5. Proprietary QSAR and generative chemistry prioritize the next compounds.
  6. ADME and toxicity predictors filter risk early.
  7. Wet-lab synthesis, assays, and structural work measure what happened.
  8. Experimental feedback updates the next design cycle.

The final arbiter is experimental feedback. A stack is valuable when it improves the next decision and learns from the result, not when it replaces the result.

01 / Atoms in motion

Every molecular trajectory depends on an energy model.

Molecular dynamics (MD) follows atom-by-atom motion through time. Unlike continuum methods, which represent a material as smooth fields, MD resolves individual atoms and uses an interatomic potential to turn each configuration into energy and forces.

02 / Five windows into MD

Different systems, the same energy → force → motion loop.

Gold nanowire stretching

Pulling the ends apart reorganizes the atomic lattice, narrows it to a tiny bridge, and eventually causes fracture. MD reveals the local rearrangements hidden by a continuum stress curve.

Copper–nickel brazing

Temperature accelerates diffusion across the interface. Trajectories let researchers estimate mixing rates and inspect how the initially sharp boundary becomes an interdiffusion zone.

Micrometeorite impact

A fast projectile transfers energy into a mineral, breaking bonds and ejecting atoms. Reactive models can examine dehydroxylation and other space-weathering chemistry.

Peptide self-assembly

Thermal motion explores many molecular arrangements; favorable contacts stabilize larger structures. The result connects local interactions to emergent morphology.

Nitrogen on iron

An electronic-structure-aware trajectory can show N₂ approaching iron, accepting charge, crossing a transition region, and dissociating into adsorbed nitrogen atoms.

1900s · pair potential

Lennard–Jones

Two parameters describe short-range repulsion and longer-range attraction as a function of pair distance. Fast and useful for simple dispersion-dominated systems, but too restrictive for realistic bonding.

1929 · flexible bond curve

Morse

An extra shape parameter improves bond stretching and compressibility relative to a simple pair curve. More flexible still does not mean many-body: coordination and angular chemistry remain missing.

1985 · explicit angles

Stillinger–Weber

A two-body term plus a three-body angular term favors tetrahedral silicon geometry. It shows how a third atom changes an interaction. 10.1103/PhysRevB.31.5262

1984 · local density

Embedded Atom Method

Neighbor contributions accumulate into a local electron-density proxy; a nonlinear embedding function converts that environment into energy. It captures metallic coordination effects. 10.1103/PhysRevB.29.6443

2007 · neural energy

Behler–Parrinello

Rotation-invariant radial and angular symmetry functions feed per-atom neural networks; atomic energies sum to the total and autodiff supplies forces. It learns the functional form from DFT data. 10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.146401

2010 · similarity and uncertainty

Gaussian Approximation Potentials

Invariant neighborhood descriptors such as bispectrum or SOAP support kernel regression over atomic environments, naturally exposing similarity-based uncertainty. Local cutoffs still limit long-range physics. 10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.136403

2018 · message passing

CGCNN

Atoms become nodes and periodic neighbor relations become edges; repeated convolution updates learned crystal representations. CGCNN marks the graph transition but is not itself a force-field formulation. 10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.145301

2022 · equivariant geometry

NequIP

Scalar, vector, and higher-order features transform predictably under rotation. Equivariance improves force learning and data efficiency, though tensor products add cost. 10.1038/s41467-022-29939-5

2022 · universal materials potential

M3GNet

Three-body graph interactions, lattice information, and learned state variables support energies, forces, and stresses across diverse materials. Broad coverage helps out of the box; distance from training data still matters. 10.1016/j.mtphys.2022.100712

For energy-conserving MD, an MLIP normally learns a differentiable invariant energy so forces obtained from its gradient transform equivariantly.

Universal atom models combine large diverse datasets with architectures that can route work across specialized representations. See FAIR Chemistry’s Universal Model for Atoms.

Beyond mechanics

Electronic-structure-aware models aim to predict charge transfer, density of states, band changes, or related observables alongside mechanics—especially valuable for catalysis and conductivity.

The strongest future system may not be one model that is always right. It may be a model that knows when to ask quantum mechanics for help.
